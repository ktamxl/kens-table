Sang Kee Restaurant (生記飯店)

灣仔

Wan Chai · Hong Kong

CuisineCantonese seafood · 粵菜・海鮮
PriceHK$300–500 per person
必MICHELIN Bib Gourmand2010–11 and 2014–2025 — twelve years

A Wan Chai seafood room that has been cooking the same salt-baked chicken for nearly fifty years.

生記飯店 sits on the third floor of a commercial block on Lockhart Road — not a street-level room, not a hotel, just a lift and a floor of round tables. It has been there long enough to be planning its fiftieth anniversary, and long enough that the staff do not ask first-timers what they want; they ask what fish looks good.

The kitchen is a Cantonese seafood kitchen of the old school: whole fish steamed plainly, prawns fried in soy, and a salt-baked chicken that the MICHELIN inspectors have singled out more than once. The suckling pig is roasted to order and carved at the table, which is why the room sounds the way it does.

It holds a Bib Gourmand — not a star — and has held one for twelve of the last fifteen years. That is the correct register for the place: excellent cooking, no ceremony, a bill that does not require a special occasion.

A Cantonese seafood room that still cooks like it is 1985.The house in one line

Why it became famous

What the city, the guides and the queues actually agree on.

  • ◆古法鹽焗雞 — salt-baked chicken, ordered by nearly every table and cited by the MICHELIN guide.
  • ◆A seafood room first: the fish tanks decide the menu more than the printed one does.
  • ◆明爐BB豬 — suckling pig roasted to order, carved in the room.
  • ◆Bib Gourmand for twelve years, which in Wan Chai is a statement about consistency rather than fashion.

Top 10 must-try

OpenRice's featured dishes for this branch, plus the salt-baked chicken the MICHELIN guide names.

Source: OpenRice 必食 + house menu

  1. 古法鹽焗雞
    Traditional Salt-Baked Chicken

    The dish the guide names. Ask for it when you book — not something to decide at the table.

  2. 明爐BB豬
    Roast Suckling Pig

    Ordered ahead, carved in the room. The crackling is the point.

  3. 豉油王炒蝦
    Prawns Fried in Master Soy Sauce

    Shell-on, wok-heavy, sweet at the edges. Eat them with your hands.

  4. 菠蘿生炒骨
    Pineapple Sweet-and-Sour Pork Ribs

    One of OpenRice's four featured dishes here. Sharper and fruitier than the usual version.

  5. 蝦籽古法扣柚皮
    Braised Pomelo Peel with Shrimp Roe

    The old Cantonese test dish: bitter peel cooked until it tastes of nothing but sauce.

  6. 金錢雞
    "Gold Coin" Chicken

    Chicken liver, fat and ginger sandwiched and roasted — the dish the fiftieth-anniversary menu kept.

  7. 白糖糕
    Steamed White Sugar Cake

    Sweet, spongy, faintly fermented. The dessert the regulars take home.

  8. 魚粥
    Fish Congee

    The other half of the house reputation — rice-smooth congee with fish folded in at the end.

  9. 咕嚕肉
    Sweet-and-Sour Pork

    The test of a Cantonese kitchen: crisp, not battered into submission.

  10. 枸杞葉炒豬肝
    Stir-Fried Pork Liver with Goji Leaves

    Bitter greens, iron-rich liver, a lot of wok heat. Order it between the heavier courses.
