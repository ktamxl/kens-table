The Chairman (大班樓)

中環

Central · Hong Kong

CuisineCantonese · 粵菜

The small Cantonese kitchen that reached No. 1 in Asia.

The Chairman opened in 2009, when Danny Yip decided that a Cantonese restaurant could be built the way a small European kitchen is built: around ingredients first, with the chef in the same room as the guests. The dining room was never designed to be grand. It was designed to be close to the cooking.

What made it different was the pantry. Instead of leaning on oyster sauce and stock cubes, the kitchen made its own soy, its own fermented bean pastes, its own preserved citrus, and bought fish and shellfish at the morning market rather than through a distributor. In Hong Kong that was close to heresy — the economics of Cantonese banquet cooking depend on scale.

The world noticed slowly, then all at once. In 2021 The Chairman became the first Chinese restaurant to take No. 1 on Asia's 50 Best Restaurants. It returned to the top of that list in 2026, and has held a MICHELIN star through both runs. Reservations now open on a fixed calendar and disappear within minutes.

A Cantonese restaurant judged by its pantry, not its chandeliers.How the Hong Kong press describes the house style

Why it became famous

What the city, the guides and the queues actually agree on.

  • ◆First Chinese restaurant to top Asia's 50 Best (2021), and No. 1 again in 2026.
  • ◆House-made sauces, ferments and preserved ingredients — a level of in-house production almost no Cantonese kitchen attempts.
  • ◆No shark fin, no abalone flex. The menu is built on river fish, crab, chicken and vegetables.
  • ◆A tiny room with a fixed booking calendar; the queue is virtual and merciless.

Top 10 must-try

OpenRice 食評 and the house menu — the crab and the ribs are the two dishes the reviews repeat.

Source: OpenRice 必食 + house menu

  1. 雞油花雕蒸花蟹配陳村粉
    Steamed Flower Crab with Huadiao, Chicken Oil and Chencun Noodles

    The signature. The rice noodles under the crab drink up the wine, roe and chicken fat; order it for the table, not for one.

  2. 話梅肉桂糖醋排骨
    Sweet-and-Sour Pork Ribs with Preserved Plum and Cassia

    A Cantonese classic rebuilt on aged plum and cassia bark rather than ketchup and sugar.

  3. 魚米粥蝦籽象拔蚌
    Geoduck with Shrimp Roe in Fish-Milk Congee

    Geoduck sliced thin, rice cooked down in fish stock. The congee is the point as much as the shellfish.

  4. 十五年陳皮牛肉獅子頭
    Fifteen-Year Tangerine Peel Beef Lion's Head

    Aged peel against slow-braised beef — the dish that most clearly shows the pantry.

  5. 樟木煙燻黑腳鵝
    Camphor-Wood Smoked Black-Foot Goose

    Smoked over camphor, skin lacquered. Order it when there are four or more of you.

  6. 蟛蜞膏柚皮
    Pomelo Peel with Crab Roe

    Bitter peel, sweet roe, a lot of patience. One of the old Cantonese dishes almost nobody else still cooks.

  7. 蝦籽柚皮
    Shrimp-Roe Pomelo Peel

    The other half of the pomelo story — braised until the peel turns silky, then dusted with shrimp roe.

  8. 子薑皮蛋
    Century Egg with Young Ginger

    A cold starter: pickled ginger cuts the cured egg. Two bites, then the crab arrives.

  9. 白飯
    Steamed Rice

    Reviewers single it out, which tells you something about the kitchen. Order it with the crab.

  10. 陳皮紅豆沙
    Tangerine-Peel Red Bean Soup

    The dessert to finish on: aged peel, sweet red bean, served warm.
