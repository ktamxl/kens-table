Australian Dairy Company (澳洲牛奶公司)

佐敦

Jordan · Hong Kong

CuisineCha chaan teng · 茶餐廳
PriceHK$50–90 per person
無No Michelin starAnd no interest in one
Show this to the waiter畀侍應睇
腿蛋治，唔烘底，唔該。
Ham and egg sandwich — please don't toast the bread.

腿蛋治 is the shorthand for 火腿蛋三文治 (ham-and-egg sandwich). 烘底 means toasted, so 唔烘底 is “no toast”. Say it in one breath and the waiter will not ask twice.

Scrambled eggs, milk pudding, and the fastest, bluntest service in Kowloon.

澳洲牛奶公司 opened in 1970 on Parkes Street, a short walk from Jordan MTR. It is not Australian and it does not sell dairy in any modern sense. What it sells is a fixed idea of breakfast: scrambled eggs, toast, macaroni soup with ham, and a bowl of steamed milk pudding.

The room runs on choreography. You are seated with strangers, your order is taken before you finish sitting down, the food arrives in under two minutes, and the bill lands while you are still eating. Regulars consider the brusqueness part of the hospitality — it is a canteen, not a café.

The one thing that has never been rushed is the egg. It is folded slowly over low heat until it is barely set, then piled on thick toast. Food writers have been trying to reproduce it for decades; the shop has never explained how.

You do not eat here. You get fed here.Regulars, roughly

Why it became famous

What the city, the guides and the queues actually agree on.

  • ◆The scrambled egg — custardy, barely set, served on thick white toast. It is the reason the queue exists.
  • ◆Three set meals and nothing else: 早餐 (until noon), 茶餐 and 快餐. Regulars call them A, B and C — pick one, then choose fried or scrambled egg.
  • ◆蛋白燉鮮奶 (steamed egg-white milk pudding) and 杏汁燉蛋 (almond custard), both made in-house daily.
  • ◆A 1970s cha chaan teng interior that has been left almost untouched.
  • ◆Cash only, no reservations, shared tables — the queue moves faster than you expect.

Top 10 must-try

Ken's standing order plus the dishes the house is known for — not a Dianping ranking.

Source: Ken's order + house menu

  1. 快餐 A／B／C（早餐・茶餐・快餐）
    Set A / B / C — Breakfast, Tea Set, Express Most ordered

    The three set meals are what almost every table orders: 早餐 until noon, 茶餐 through the afternoon, 快餐 all day. Each is a fixed plate — bread, egg, a bowl of soup noodles — with a hot drink. The only real decisions are fried or scrambled egg, and tea or coffee.

  2. 火腿蛋三文治（唔烘底）
    Ham and Egg Sandwich — bread not toasted Ken's order

    Ken's standing order: 腿蛋治，唔烘底. Say it exactly like this — the bread stays soft. The crusts come off automatically here, so there is nothing else to add.

  3. 熱奶茶
    Hot Hong Kong Milk Tea Drink

    Ordered simply as 熱奶茶 — strong tea, evaporated milk, no foam. If you want it sweetened with condensed milk instead of sugar, ask for 茶走.

  4. 炒蛋多士
    Scrambled Eggs on Toast

    The house icon: barely-set egg folded over thick toast. It already comes inside the sets; order it à la carte only if you are skipping the set.

  5. 法蘭西多士（西多）
    French Toast Sweet

    Deep-fried, buttered, syrup on the side. Ordered in shorthand as 西多. Share it — after a set meal it is a lot of sweetness for one person.

  6. 花生醬多士
    Peanut Butter Toast Toast

    Thick white bread, a heavy scrape of peanut butter. Ask for 奶醬多 if you want peanut butter and condensed milk together — the classic combination.

  7. 煉奶多士
    Condensed Milk Toast Toast

    Warm toast with sweetened condensed milk poured over. Simpler than the French toast and, for many regulars, the better order.

  8. 蛋白燉鮮奶
    Steamed Egg-White Milk Pudding Dessert

    Served warm or cold; warm is the correct answer. Soft, faintly sweet, and the thing to order when you want to sit for five more minutes.

  9. 杏汁燉蛋
    Almond Custard Dessert

    Fragrant, softly set, and the item most first-timers forget. Order it alongside the milk pudding and share both.

  10. 火腿通粉
    Ham and Macaroni Soup Set staple

    The savoury anchor of every set — clear broth, elbow macaroni, a slice of ham. Not fashionable; entirely correct.

From the house menu and Ken's standing order. The sets are the cheapest way to eat here; the sandwich and the tea are ordered in cha chaan teng shorthand — the exact line is in the order card above.
