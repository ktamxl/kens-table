Yue Xing Qi Seafood Dai Pai Dong (粤兴起超级海鲜大排档)

二沙岛店

Ersha Island / Yanjiang Road · Guangzhou

CuisineCantonese seafood, dai pai dong · 海鲜・大排档
Price¥105–200 per person
1No. 1 Seafood Restaurant for Taste, Ersha IslandDianping 口味榜
4.8Dianping 4.8 / 51,907 reviews at the time of listing
荐Locals Recommend962 local reviews — 当地人推荐

A seafood dai pai dong on Ersha Island, open until 1:30 in the morning.

粤兴起 is a 大排档 in the proper sense: tanks at the front, plastic stools, woks going hard, and a crowd that arrives after nine and stays past midnight. It sits on Qingbo Road on Ersha Island, a hundred and fifty metres from Haixinsha Park, and it opens at five in the evening and closes at half past one.

The seafood is the point. Mantis shrimp the length of a forearm, Sri Lankan crab under a mountain of fried garlic, a six-catty Boston lobster wok-fried with ginger and scallion. Prices are by weight for the big items, so ask before the tank bag closes.

It is No. 1 on Ersha Island for seafood taste, 4.8 across 1,907 reviews, and — unusually — reviewed by locals rather than tourists: 962 local recommendations.

The non-seafood dishes are not an afterthought. The honey roast goose, the sizzling chicken claypot and the ¥38 fried rice noodles are what the regulars order alongside the shellfish.

Ask the weight before the tank bag closes. Then order the mantis shrimp anyway.How to order

Why it became famous

What the city, the guides and the queues actually agree on.

  • ◆Arm-length Thai mantis shrimp, cut at the table.
  • ◆No. 1 seafood restaurant for taste on Ersha Island, with 962 local recommendations.
  • ◆Open until 01:30 — the late-night seafood room in this part of the city.
  • ◆Big-ticket crab and lobster priced by weight, cooked Cantonese-style rather than drowned in sauce.

Top 10 must-try

Dianping 推荐菜, TOP 1–10 for the Ersha Island branch — ranked by the app's like counts.

Source: Dianping 推荐菜 · TOP 1–10

  1. 中号泰国手臂爆肉濑尿虾
    Thai Arm-Sized Mantis Shrimp ¥188 No. 1

    The signature: a mantis shrimp the length of your forearm, cut open at the table so you can pull the meat out in one piece.

  2. 啫啫走地鸡
    Sizzling Free-Range Chicken ¥78 No. 2

    Claypot chicken with ginger and spring onion, still bubbling when it lands. 700+ sold in six months.

  3. 兴起蜜汁烧鹅
    Yue Xing Qi Honey Roast Goose ¥98 No. 3

    Honey-glazed, roasted dark, skin crisp under the glaze. The non-seafood order everyone still makes.

  4. 美极爆膏罗氏虾
    Prawns with Roe in Maggi Sauce ¥138 No. 4

    Prawns split and flash-fried in Maggi, roe still soft. 1,000+ sold in six months — the most-ordered dish in volume.

  5. 鸡蛋炒米粉
    Egg Fried Rice Noodles ¥38 No. 5

    Wok-fried rice noodles with egg and bean sprouts. The cheap order that soaks up everything else.

  6. 姜葱炒波龙
    Boston Lobster with Ginger and Scallion ¥298 No. 6

    A six-catty lobster wok-fried with ginger and scallion — the classic Cantonese treatment, no cheese, no sauce.

  7. 避风塘斯里兰卡蟹
    Typhoon-Shelter Sri Lankan Crab ¥750 No. 7

    The showpiece: crab buried under fried garlic and chilli. Priced by weight — confirm before it goes in the tank.

  8. 怀旧咕噜肉
    Nostalgic Sweet-and-Sour Pork ¥58 No. 8

    Crisp shell, hot pineapple, sauce balanced sharp rather than sugary. The dish that tells you the wok is hot.

  9. 蒜蓉生蚝
    Grilled Oysters with Garlic ¥19.8 No. 9

    Fat oysters under garlic, grilled until the edges curl. At ¥19.8, order a round for the table.

  10. 椒盐牛蛙
    Salt-and-Pepper Bullfrog ¥68 No. 10

    Crisp, peppery, springy. 800+ sold in six months, and the best beer food on the menu.

Prices from the Dianping listing. Crab and lobster are market-priced by weight — confirm at the table. The 100-yuan voucher sells for ¥87 and up to ten can be used per table.
