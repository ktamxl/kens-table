Yung Kee Roast Goose (榕記陳皮燒鵝)

尖沙咀金巴利道

Tsim Sha Tsui · Hong Kong

CuisineCantonese roast meats · 粵式燒味
PriceHK$80–200 per person
金HKTB Char Siu Gold AwardFor 榕哥's lemongrass and black-pepper char siu
馬China Restaurant Golden Horse AwardA chef's award, not a venue rating

Roast goose marinated with aged tangerine peel — a Kimberley Road shop, not the Central institution.

榕記陳皮燒鵝 is the shop of 榕哥, a Cantonese roast-meat chef who built his reputation on two things: char siu and goose. His lemongrass-and-black-pepper char siu took a gold award from the Hong Kong Tourism Board, and he has collected the China Restaurant Golden Horse Award and a Hong Kong Top Ten Chefs title along the way. The restaurant under his own name opened in 2016.

The goose is the reason to come. He uses female black-brown geese of around five and a half catties — smaller birds, less fat — and rubs them with aged tangerine peel among a dozen other aromatics before roasting. The peel does something smoke cannot: it cuts the fat so the skin tastes bright rather than heavy.

One warning about the name. This is 榕記 on Kimberley Road, not 鏞記酒家 on Wellington Street in Central. The English romanisation is identical and the two are constantly confused; they are separate businesses, with separate menus and separate rooms. If you want the 1942 Central institution, book there. If you want the goose with the tangerine peel, come here.

Old tangerine peel cuts the fat. That is the whole trick.The house technique

Why it became famous

What the city, the guides and the queues actually agree on.

  • ◆陳皮燒鵝 — goose marinated with aged tangerine peel, the house signature.
  • ◆A char siu that won a Hong Kong Tourism Board gold award for its lemongrass and black-pepper marinade.
  • ◆Goose leg on rice for around HK$138 — the order most tables make.
  • ◆A chef-led shop rather than a chain: 榕哥 still runs the roasting.

Top 10 must-try

The roast-meat counter at this Kimberley Road shop, plus the dishes reviewers return for.

Source: OpenRice 必食 + house menu

  1. 陳皮燒鵝
    Aged Tangerine-Peel Roast Goose

    The signature. Ask for the leg if ordering by portion; the breast is leaner but less interesting.

  2. 燒鵝脾飯
    Roast Goose Leg on Rice

    The single order. Sauce on the side so the skin stays crisp.

  3. 金獎傳統叉燒
    Award-Winning Traditional Char Siu

    Lemongrass and black pepper in the marinade — sweeter and more fragrant than the usual honey glaze.

  4. 紅燒乳鴿
    Roast Pigeon

    Small, dark, best eaten with your hands. A regular add-on.

  5. 乾炒牛河
    Dry-Fried Beef Ho Fun

    The wok-hei test of any Cantonese kitchen. Order it if you are sharing.

  6. 滷水鵝掌
    Braised Goose Webs

    Cold, gelatinous, made for beer.

  7. 脆皮燒肉
    Crispy Roast Pork

    Added to a goose plate rather than ordered alone.

  8. 鵝油撈麵
    Noodles Tossed in Goose Fat

    The best use of the fat that comes off the birds.

  9. 例湯
    Soup of the Day

    Usually pork bone and vegetable. Included with most rice plates.

  10. 油菜
    Blanched Seasonal Greens

    Oyster sauce on the side. The counterweight to a plate of roast meat.
