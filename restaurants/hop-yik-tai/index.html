Hop Yik Tai (合益泰小食)

深水埗

Sham Shui Po · Hong Kong

CuisineStreet snacks and congee · 街頭小食・粥品
PriceHK$20–60 per person
米MICHELIN RecommendedStreet-food listing
Show this to the waiter畀侍應睇
腸粉，唔該。 碎牛粥，加豬紅。
Rice noodle rolls, please. / Minced-beef congee, with pork blood.

Order the 腸粉 at the street counter. If you take a seat inside, the congee is the reason — 碎牛 is minced beef, 豬紅 is pork blood.

How to eat it食法

Minced beef congee, in five steps

  1. The minced beef is raw, pressed flat against the bottom of the bowl before it leaves the kitchen.
  2. Boiling congee is poured over it at the pass — the heat of the congee cooks the beef, not the stove.
  3. Fried rice noodles are folded through. That is the signature of this bowl: crisp noodle inside a soft congee.
  4. Take your soup spoon and stab straight down to the bottom to break the beef apart.
  5. Do not stir. Stirring liquefies the congee and turns it watery; stabbing keeps the starch thick.

Five thousand rice noodles a day, and a bowl of congee you have to know how to eat.

合益泰 is a hole-in-the-wall on Kweilin Street with a queue that starts before the shutters are fully up. The counter sells 腸粉 — rice noodle rolls steamed to order, cut into lengths and dressed with sweet sauce, sesame paste and soy — at a rate of roughly five thousand a day. You eat them standing, from a paper plate, with a bamboo skewer.

Behind the counter there is a seating area, and that is where the congee happens. 碎牛粥 is the order: minced beef pressed raw into the bottom of the bowl, boiling congee poured over it, fried rice noodles folded through. The beef cooks in the bowl. The noodles stay crisp for about a minute.

The shop is Michelin-recommended and has been for years. It closed for four months and reopened in early 2026 after a refit — the queue came back the same week.

Stab, do not stir. The congee stays thick that way.The house instruction

Why it became famous

What the city, the guides and the queues actually agree on.

  • ◆腸粉 sold by the thousand from a street counter — cheap, fast, eaten standing up.
  • ◆碎牛粥 with the beef cooked by the congee itself, and fried rice noodles folded through.
  • ◆A MICHELIN street-food recommendation in a district full of them.
  • ◆Reopened in early 2026 after a four-month refit; the queue never really left.

Top 10 must-try

The counter's rice noodles plus the seated menu — the congee is the reason to stay.

Source: OpenRice 必食 + house menu

  1. 豬油腸粉
    Rice Noodle Rolls with Lard

    The street-counter order. Sweet sauce, sesame paste, soy — do not ask for it dry.

  2. 碎牛粥
    Minced Beef Congee

    The seated order. Read the ritual above before you touch it.

  3. 豆漿
    Soy Milk

    Cold or warm, unsweetened by default. The thing to drink with the noodles.

  4. 燒賣
    Steamed Siu Mai

    Fish-based, not pork, and served with the same sweet-soy dressing.

  5. 豬皮蘿蔔
    Pork Skin and Radish

    Braised in a curry-ish broth; order it if you are sharing.

  6. 魚蛋
    Fish Balls

    Springy, cheap, and best with the same sauce as the noodles.

  7. 牛肉球
    Beef Balls

    Steamed, plain, made for dipping.

  8. 油條
    Fried Dough Stick

    For the congee, not on its own.

  9. 白粥
    Plain Congee

    The control against which the beef version makes sense.

  10. 齋腸
    Plain Rice Noodle Roll

    No filling, just the noodle and the sauces — the purest test of the counter.
